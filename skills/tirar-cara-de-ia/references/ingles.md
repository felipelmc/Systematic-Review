# AI-writing tells — English

Use this as a recognition map, **not** a blind ban-list. Almost every single
term below is fine in isolation; what gives away the machine is *frequency*,
*uniformity*, and *accumulation* — the same devices deployed relentlessly and
symmetrically, plus the absence of concrete, specific content. Prefer cutting
over swapping in an odd synonym.

## 1. Lexical tells

### 1.1 Overused verbs
The single strongest lexical category — empirical studies find AI "excess
vocabulary" is ~66% verbs.

delve (into) · leverage · harness · underscore · foster · streamline · utilize ·
facilitate · optimize · embark · empower · elevate · unleash · unlock · bolster ·
amplify · revolutionize · transform · illuminate · cultivate · navigate ·
unravel · elucidate · encompass · discern · showcase · boast · garner · surpass ·
align · mitigate · safeguard · pioneer · unveil · captivate · resonate · propel ·
pave (the way) · shed (light on) · craft · dive (into) · tap into · spearhead ·
ignite · uncover · demystify · redefine · reimagine · curate.

**Verb inflation** (a named tell): AI upgrades plain verbs — *use → leverage/
utilize · make → craft · help → empower · show → showcase · start → embark on ·
improve → elevate/enhance.*

## 2. Filler transitions

Opening sentences mechanically: **Moreover · Furthermore · Additionally · In
addition · However · Nevertheless · Nonetheless · Therefore · Thus ·
Consequently · Subsequently · That said · With that in mind · It's worth noting
that · It's important to note/remember that · As previously mentioned · Notably ·
Importantly · In essence · Ultimately · In conclusion · To sum up · All in all ·
At the end of the day · Firstly / Secondly / Finally.**

Quick test: delete the transition. If the two sentences still connect clearly, it
was filler — leave it deleted. Prefer meaning-carrying subordination (*because,
since, although*) over decorative adverbials (*moreover, furthermore*).

## 3. Cliché phrases and stock sentences

**Openers / hooks:** "In today's fast-paced world…" · "In today's rapidly
evolving digital landscape…" · "In a world where…" · "In an era characterized
by…" · "In the digital age…" · "In the realm of…" · "As we navigate…" · "Picture
this:" · "Imagine a world where…" · "Let's dive in / delve into / unpack this" ·
"Have you ever wondered…"

**Closers:** "In conclusion…" · "In summary…" · "To sum up…" · "Overall…" ·
"Ultimately…" · "At the end of the day…" · "In essence…" · "Only time will
tell." · "The possibilities are endless." · "One thing is clear:…" · "Whether
you're X or Y, [product] has something for everyone."

**Editorializing / announcing significance:** "It's important to note that…" ·
"It's worth noting that…" · "It bears mentioning…" · "Notably," · "Interestingly,"
· "No discussion of X would be complete without…" · "Needless to say…"

**Hedging stock phrases:** "Generally speaking" · "It could be argued that…" ·
"One might argue that…" · "While it is true that…" · "It depends on various
factors" · "Both sides have valid points" · "to some extent" · "tends to."

**"Importance/role" formulas:** "plays a pivotal / vital / crucial role" · "serves
as a testament to" · "stands as a testament to" · "leaves a lasting impact" ·
"underscores the importance of" · "highlights the significance of" · "a
cornerstone of."

**Chatbot-artifact phrases (near-definitive tells if they survive into final
copy):** "As an AI language model…" · "As of my last knowledge update…" · "I do
not have personal opinions/experiences…" · "Based on the information provided…"

**Sycophantic / service phrases leaking into prose:** "Great question!" ·
"Absolutely!" · "Certainly! Here's…" · "I hope this helps! Let me know if you
have any other questions." · "I hope this message finds you well."

## 4. Syntactic and rhetorical constructions

- **Negative parallelism / antithesis ("It's not X, it's Y")** — the most-
  discussed structural tell. Variants: *"It's not just X — it's Y," "This isn't
  about X; it's about Y," "X — not Y," "The question isn't X. The question is Y."*
  Example: "It's not a product launch. It's a paradigm shift." → State Y directly.
- **"Not X. Not Y. Just Z."** — dramatic countdown before a reveal.
- **Tricolon / rule of three** — traits, adjectives, or clauses in threes:
  *"clear, concise, and compelling."* Often ascending (short→long). The tell is
  reflexive triads whether or not reality has three items.
- **Adjectival clustering** — three near-synonyms: *"efficient, reliable, and
  effective."*
- **Rhetorical Q&A / self-answered questions** — "The result? Devastating." ·
  "Why does this matter? Because…" · "So what does this mean for you?"
- **False ranges ("From X to Y")** — endpoints not on a real spectrum: *"From
  startups to enterprises," "From intimate gatherings to global movements."*
- **Participial "-ing" tag-ons (superficial analysis)** — a trailing clause that
  fakes depth: *"…, underscoring its role as a dynamic hub," "…, highlighting its
  lasting influence."*
- **"Serves as / stands as / represents" instead of "is"** — the copula dodge.
- **Anaphora abuse** — repeated sentence openings: *"They assume users will pay…
  They assume developers will build…"*
- **Staccato fragments for drama** — clusters of very short standalone lines.
- **"Here's the kicker" / false-suspense pivots** — "Here's the thing," "Here's
  where it gets interesting," "But here's the kicker."
- **"Listicle in a trench coat"** — prose that's secretly a list: *"The first…
  The second… The third…"*
- **Patronizing analogy ("Think of it as…")** — "Think of it like a highway
  system for data."
- **"Imagine a world where…"** — futuristic wish-list framing.
- **"Despite its challenges…" redemption formula** — raise a problem, then
  dismiss it into optimism.
- **Invented concept labels** — made-up compounds presented as established terms:
  *"the supervision paradox," "the acceleration trap."*
- **Historical-analogy stacking** — rapid-fire references for borrowed authority.

## 5. Punctuation and formatting

- **Em-dash overuse ("the ChatGPT hyphen")** — compulsive `—` where humans use
  commas, parentheses, or periods. *Note: the least reliable single tell — many
  human stylists love it, and models tune it down on request. The tell is
  frequency, not the mark.*
- **Colon addiction** — dramatic setup colons everywhere: *"The result: chaos."*
- **Semicolon fondness** — grammatically perfect semicolons at an atypical rate.
- **"Smart"/curly quotes** where hand-typed text usually has straight quotes;
  **missing en dashes** in ranges (pages 12–18).
- **Bold-lead bullets** — every item opens with a bolded phrase and a colon:
  **`Security:`** … **`Scalability:`** …
- **Decorative mid-paragraph bolding** that emphasizes nothing.
- **Emoji as structure** — ✅ 🚀 💡 in headers/bullets, esp. in professional copy.
- **Unicode arrows** — "Input → Processing → Output."
- **Fragment-as-paragraph** and **over-listing** (bullets where prose belongs).
- **Rigid Title Case headings** and uniform, symmetric heading hierarchy.
- **Section-summary "fractals"** — recap at every level (intro previews, each
  section restates itself, conclusion re-summarizes the intro).

## 6. Tone and structural markers

- **Relentless positivity / uniform enthusiasm** — everything is "exciting,"
  "powerful"; same energy for trivial and major points.
- **Neutral, non-committal stance** — hedging and "both sides" balance that
  avoids any strong or novel claim.
- **Low burstiness** — uniform sentence length and rhythm; humans alternate long
  and short.
- **Low perplexity** — statistically predictable word choices; few surprising
  collocations or idioms.
- **Symmetrical paragraph molds** — near-identical length and internal shape
  (claim → example → significance), repeated.
- **Semantic hollowness** — grammatically flawless sentences that sound
  authoritative while saying little; "polished but shallow."
- **One-point dilution** — a single idea restated many ways; near-verbatim
  content duplication.
- **Vague attribution** — "Studies show," "Experts say," "Research suggests" with
  no named source.
- **Performative vulnerability** — "I'll be honest," "Let's be real."
- **Collaborative "let's" framing** in one-directional text — "Let's break this
  down," "Let's unpack this."

## 7. Notes for academic writing

Formality, impersonal voice, calibrated hedging, and IMRaD signposting are
legitimate — do **not** strip them. The targets are:

- **Excess "style" vocabulary** (empirically the biggest signal): *delve(s),
  showcasing, underscore(s), intricate, pivotal, crucial, comprehensive,
  meticulous, commendable, noteworthy, realm, garner, boast, multifaceted,
  holistic, nuanced.*
- **Formulaic scaffolding** — "This study aims to…," "plays a crucial role in…,"
  "sheds light on…," "a comprehensive understanding of…," "further research is
  needed," "challenges and future directions."
- **Vague attribution instead of real citations** — a field defined by citation
  exposes "studies show" instantly. Replace with a named citation; if the author
  didn't supply the source, **flag the gap — never fabricate a reference**
  (fabricated/misattributed citations are a near-definitive AI tell).
- **Over-hedging by volume** and the reflexive **hedge-then-reassure** pair,
  rather than evidence-calibrated hedging.
- **Weak argumentation** — heavy summarization, little engagement with
  counter-arguments; oversimplified conclusions.

> Note: AI detectors are *worse* on scientific writing, because legitimate
> scientific style overlaps with AI's flat register — false positives are highest
> exactly here. Lean on citation integrity and specificity, not "vibe."

## 8. Notes for professional/corporate writing

Bullets, executive summaries, headers, and scannable structure are *functional*
in skim-first documents — not tells. The targets are:

- **Buzzword bingo** — synergy · paradigm shift · leverage · scalable · robust ·
  end-to-end · best practices · actionable insights · drive innovation · unlock
  scalable growth · value-add · move the needle · north star · best-in-class.
- **Verb inflation** (use→leverage, make→craft).
- **Manufactured hooks** — "In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape…"
- **Letter/email templates bleeding in** — "I hope this message finds you well,"
  "Thank you for your time and consideration."
- **Fake experience / E-E-A-T tells** — generic "As a business owner, you
  know…," "Picture this…" with no specific, verifiable detail.
- **Uniform register + risk aversion** — no controversial claim, no named
  failure, no real number; "generic specificity" instead of the actual client,
  metric, or outcome.

> Emails and proposals read human when they're **direct and specific**: what,
> when, who, how much. Swap "significantly optimize workflows" for the concrete
> gain ("cut invoice processing from 5 days to 2"). Restore a recommendation:
> "I recommend X because Y" reads human; excessive neutrality is an AI tell.

## 9. Empirical detection findings (named markers)

- **"Excess vocabulary" in academic corpora (Kobak et al., 2024).** ~280 excess
  "style" words in 2024 PubMed abstracts; *delves* ≈25×, *showcasing* ≈9.2×,
  *underscores* ≈9.1× more frequent post-ChatGPT. 2024's excess words were ~66%
  verbs / ~18% adjectives (style, not topic). Lower-bound ≥10% of abstracts
  LLM-processed.
- **"Delve" case study.** Flat through 2022, surged to ~7.9 per 1,000 papers by
  Q1 2024; ~46% of all 1990–2024 papers using "delve" appeared in the 15 months
  after ChatGPT launched.
- **PubMed study (2024).** 75 terms with modified Z-score ≥3.5 in 2024 —
  *underscore, meticulous, commendable, showcase, intricate, tapestry, realm,
  cutting-edge, groundbreaking, pivotal, holistic, transformative, testament,
  seamless, streamline, harness, comprehensive*, and more.
- **Pangram phrase frequencies (AI vs human):** "As an AI language model"
  ~294,000×; "vibrant tapestry" ~17,000×; "serves as a testament" ~4,000×.
- **Why these words?** Best-supported cause is RLHF: human raters use certain
  "impressive" words as quality proxies, so the model overproduces them;
  reinforced by mode collapse (gravitation to high-probability sequences).
- **Perplexity & burstiness.** AI text is low-perplexity (predictable) and
  low-burstiness (little sentence-to-sentence variation). But these are *weak*
  detectors — see `estrutura-retorica-e-deteccao.md`. Do not optimize *for* them.
- **Reliability caveat.** Educated readers misidentify AI text ~50% of the time;
  detectors produce false positives (esp. for non-native writers and science
  prose); **no single feature — least of all the em dash — is dispositive.**

## 10. Before / after

**Hype + vague →**
- Before: "Our innovative solution seamlessly optimizes workflows, delivering
  robust results and an unparalleled experience."
- After: "Our tool cuts invoice processing from 5 days to 2."

**Negative parallelism + tricolon →**
- Before: "It's not just about technology — it's about people, purpose, and
  progress."
- After: "The technology only pays off when the team actually uses it."

**Filler transition + signposted conclusion →**
- Before: "Moreover, it's worth noting that the methodology matters. In
  conclusion, it is clear that it should be followed."
- After: "The methodology matters because [concrete reason]."

**Academic vagueness →**
- Before: "Studies show that inequality significantly affects democracy."
- After: "Acemoglu and Robinson (2012) argue that income concentration weakens
  representative institutions." *(If that source isn't in the author's material,
  flag the gap instead of writing this.)*
